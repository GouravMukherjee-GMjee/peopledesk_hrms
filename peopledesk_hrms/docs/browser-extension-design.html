Browser extension widget

See your clock-in time, expected logout and time left in the browser, without opening PeopleDesk. Controlled by the admin per role, then by each person's own opt-in. This page is the design spec; every popup, badge and notification below is rendered by the same code the real extension runs.

3. Toolbar icon and badge

The badge shows the time left: 5h while an hour or more remains, then 42m. Hover shows the tooltip. Every state differs by text or by a small shape on the icon, never by colour alone.

7. Accessibility annotations

TopicSpec
ContrastWCAG AA. Body text 4.5:1 or better, large text and UI edges 3:1. Badge colours: white on green, blue, purple and grey; dark text on amber. Checked for light and dark.
Not colour aloneEach state has a text label in the popup, a glyph in the status chip, different badge text (5h, 42m, Done, +25m, ?) and a different shape on the toolbar icon.
KeyboardTab order follows reading order: status, primary action, secondary action. Focus starts on the first button when the popup opens. Enter or Space activates. Escape closes the popup or dialog. The tooltip on “Hide for today” opens on focus as well as hover. Dialogs trap focus and return it to the opener. Settings radios use arrow keys.
Labels (ARIA)Switches are role="switch" with a name such as “Show in browser”. Progress bar: role="progressbar", aria-valuenow and aria-valuetext “62 percent of the day. 5 hours 12 minutes left”. Lock icon: aria-label="Not available for your role", focusable, linked to its tooltip with aria-describedby. Status banners use role="status"; errors role="alert". Preview frames are role="img" with a text alternative and are inert. Setup stepper marks aria-current="step".
Live updatesThe countdown changes once a minute and is not an aria-live region, so screen readers are not interrupted. Reminders arrive as notifications.
Reduced motionWith prefers-reduced-motion, the loading shimmer, bar growth and tooltip fades are removed. Nothing flashes or auto-plays.
Touch and zoomTargets are at least 40px tall. Layout holds at 200% zoom; admin screens collapse to one column under 1100px.
LanguagePlain, friendly, neutral. “Hide for today”, never “Cancel”. “Your attendance is not affected” is always one hover away.

8. Design rationale

  • Two gates, one rule. The admin decides what is possible and the person decides what they want. Showing the rule in plain words (“this role allows it and they have opted in”) avoids support tickets about “why can't I see it”.
  • Locked, not hidden. Options a role cannot use stay visible with a lock and “Not available for your role”. Hiding them makes people think something is broken.
  • Glanceable in two seconds. One 44px number (expected logout), one bar, one label. Clock-in time and the source tag are secondary. Extra time replaces the countdown instead of adding a second number.
  • “Hide for today”, not “Cancel” or “Close”. Those words sound like they cancel attendance. The tooltip says it does not.
  • Minute, not second. A ticking seconds counter in the corner of someone's browser feels like surveillance and wastes battery. The badge and popup move once a minute.
  • Attendance only, stated everywhere. The prompt, the admin page and the preferences all repeat it. The extension asks for no website access and its manifest asks only for storage, alarms and notifications.
  • Calm colour. Green for working, amber for “under 30 minutes”, blue for done, purple for extra time. Overtime is purple, not red, so it never reads as a warning or a fault.
  • Works when the app is closed. The extension keeps the clock-in and expected logout and counts down by itself. It does not need PeopleDesk open.
  • Honest offline. When data may be old, the popup dims it and says “Last updated 10:30” rather than showing a confident wrong number.
  • Reminders are opt-in and bounded. Each shows once a day, the admin sets the menu, the person picks from it, and “Pause until tomorrow” stops all of it without turning it off.
