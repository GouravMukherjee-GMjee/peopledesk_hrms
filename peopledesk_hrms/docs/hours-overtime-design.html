Hours, overtime and comp-off

One place for an employee to see how long they worked, what was extra, what it is worth and how to use it. Every screenshot below is the real module running in PeopleDesk, with the app's sample data. Open it from Time → Hours & overtime. Admin rules are in Settings → Overtime and comp-off.

Default rules (editable assumptions)

Nothing is hard-coded. Each rule below is a field in Settings → Overtime and comp-off, saved with a change history in the audit log, and can be overridden per role or department.

Accessibility notes

TopicWhat was done
ContrastWCAG AA in light and dark. Tinted cards use the dark ink of their own tone, not grey.
Not colour aloneStatuses are words. Short days carry ▼ and the minutes. Overtime is hatched on the chart and marked + on the calendar. Holiday work, Leave and Absent are written in the cell.
Charts have a tableUnder the week chart and the month calendar is a View as a table section with the same numbers. Every bar and cell is a button with a full sentence label, for example “Wed 28 Oct 2026, Working day, 10h 15m worked, 8h 15m expected, 2h 00m overtime. Open details”.
KeyboardEverything is a real button, link or input. The period switcher and chips use aria-pressed. Dialogs and drawers trap focus, close with Escape and return focus. The calendar for comp-off dates uses buttons you can tab to, and unavailable days say why.
Labels (ARIA)Progress uses role="progressbar" with text values. The stepper marks aria-current="step". Comp-off and pay are a radiogroup. The running total and the selection count are aria-live. Errors use role="alert".
Reduced motionThe loading shimmer stops and transitions are removed under prefers-reduced-motion.
NumbersTabular figures and one format everywhere: Xh Ym, with a real minus sign for negatives.
TouchTargets are at least 36px. The phone layout stacks cards, turns tables into labelled cards and moves tabs into a menu.

Design rationale

  • The answer comes first. Four cards sit directly under the period switcher: worked, expected, difference, overtime. Nothing else competes with them, and they change with Today, This week and This month.
  • Hours, status and payout month travel together. Wherever an amount appears (table row, drawer, flow step, request card) the hours, the status chip and the payroll month are beside it. There are no surprises at payroll.
  • Overtime and holiday work are separate on purpose. They have different rates, different expectations and a different story (“I gave up my day off”). Keeping them apart avoids a confusing mixed total.
  • Estimates are labelled as estimates. “Estimate, confirmed by payroll” appears next to every amount.
  • Make the policy visible, not secret. The rules are listed as editable assumptions, and each locked option explains itself (“Your policy allows extra pay only”).
  • Errors are placed where the decision is made. Not enough hours, the payroll cutoff and a locked policy appear inside the step they affect, with what to do next.
  • Expiry is a warning, not a surprise. A banner 14 days ahead, a notification, and the expiry date in every ledger row.
  • Nothing is lost mid-flow. The flow can go back, keeps your picks, and a pending request can be cancelled, which returns the hours.
  • The same record serves everyone. The employee, the manager and the admin all work from one list of entries and one policy, so numbers never disagree.
  • Friendly wording. “Auto-calculated”, “Request approval” and “Raise a query”. Never “violation” or “unauthorised”.
